Tea generally has less caffeine than coffee, and it's easier to step down gradually with tea. Here's roughly what you'd get in an 8 oz (240 ml) cup:

| Drink | Caffeine |
|---|---|
| Brewed coffee | 80–100 mg |
| Black tea | 40–70 mg |
| Oolong | 30–50 mg |
| Green tea | 25–35 mg |
| White tea | 15–30 mg |
| Decaf coffee | 2–15 mg |
| Herbal tea (chamomile, peppermint, rooibos) | 0 mg |

These are ballpark figures. Brew strength, steeping time, and cup size change them a lot.

**Why tea helps:**
- You can fine-tune it. Caffeine comes out early in steeping, so a shorter steep or less leaf means less caffeine.
- There's a natural ladder: black, oolong, green, white, then herbal. Each step is a clear cut.
- Tea contains L-theanine, which many people find makes caffeine feel smoother and less jittery. The evidence for that is modest, but it's a common experience.

**If you want to keep coffee:**
- Half-caf (equal parts regular and decaf) is an easy middle step, and many people find decaf tastes close enough to keep the routine.
- Size matters. A single espresso shot has roughly 60–75 mg, less than an 8 oz drip, but a double-shot latte or a large cold brew can carry much more.

**Watch for:**
- Not all tea is low. Matcha has about 60–70 mg per teaspoon, close to a cup of coffee, and strong black tea and chai can be high too.
- "Herbal" doesn't always mean caffeine-free. Yerba mate and guayusa both contain caffeine.
- Caffeine also comes from chocolate, cola, some pain relievers, and energy drinks.

**A practical approach:** Cut back gradually rather than stopping cold, since quitting abruptly often brings headaches and fatigue for several days. Also set a cutoff about six to eight hours before bed. Caffeine's half-life averages around five hours but varies a lot from person to person.

If you love coffee, decaf or half-caf keeps the habit while cutting most of the caffeine. For a bigger reduction, move toward green, white, or herbal tea.
