**Light Left On**

The bottle rolled up out of the kelp at low tide, its cork swollen with salt. Arthur carried it up the forty-two steps to the lamp room, where morning sun came through the glass.

Inside was a sheet of blue-lined paper, folded twice.

*My name is Ivy. I live across the island with my gran. She says the lighthouse is empty, but I see its light at night. Is anyone there?*

Arthur read it three times, then found his late wife's pen in the kitchen drawer and wrote on the back of his tide tables.

*Dear Ivy, I am here. I have kept the lamp lit every night for thirty years. Your gran is right that the tower is old, but it isn't empty.*

He sealed the reply in a marmalade jar and threw it from the cliff as far as he could.

For nine days he kept the lamp burning and watched the water.

On the tenth morning, a jar knocked against the rocks. Inside, in purple crayon, was one line:

*Gran says you must be very lonely. I said you're probably just busy. Write again?*

Arthur laughed, alone in the lamp room, and went down for the pen.
