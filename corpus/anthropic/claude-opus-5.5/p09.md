Cup for cup, tea has a lot less caffeine than coffee. Black tea typically has about half as much, and green tea about a third. That makes swapping some or all of your coffee for tea one of the easiest ways to cut back while keeping the habit of a warm drink.

**Typical caffeine per 8 oz (240 mL) cup**

| Drink | Caffeine |
|---|---|
| Brewed coffee | 80–150 mg |
| Espresso (per shot) | ~65 mg |
| Black tea | 40–70 mg |
| Green tea | 20–45 mg |
| Matcha (per serving) | 60–80 mg |
| Decaf coffee | 2–15 mg |
| Herbal tea (rooibos, chamomile, peppermint) | 0 mg |

Real amounts vary a lot with brew strength, steep time and cup size, so treat these as ballparks.

**Where they differ**

- **You can make tea weaker easily.** A shorter steep, say 2 minutes instead of 5, pulls out less caffeine. That lets you taper without changing what you drink. One myth to skip: rinsing tea leaves for 30 seconds doesn't decaffeinate them, because it removes only a small fraction.
- **Tea often feels gentler.** It contains L-theanine, an amino acid that may soften caffeine's effects. Many people describe tea as giving a calmer, less jittery lift. The research hints at this without proving it, but it fits what a lot of tea drinkers report.
- **Coffee has the better decaf.** Decent decaf coffee is easy to find and tastes much closer to the real thing than it used to. Decaf tea exists too, but many people prefer herbal teas as their caffeine-free option.
- **Watch the exceptions.** With matcha you consume the whole powdered leaf, so it has more caffeine than regular green tea. Matcha and strongly brewed breakfast teas can come close to a weak cup of coffee. Yerba mate is often sold alongside herbal teas, but it does contain caffeine.

**Tips for cutting back**

1. **Taper instead of quitting all at once.** Stopping suddenly can bring a few days of headaches, fatigue and irritability. Replacing one cup every few days usually avoids most of that.
2. **Swap by time of day.** Keep your morning coffee if you love it. Switch to green tea in the early afternoon, then herbal tea or decaf after that. Caffeine's half-life averages around 5 hours, so roughly half the caffeine from a 3 p.m. coffee is still in your system at 8 p.m.
3. **Try half-caf.** Mixing regular and decaf beans halves the caffeine without much change in flavor.
4. **Mind the size.** A large coffee-shop coffee can easily hit 300 mg or more. Ordering a smaller size cuts a lot of caffeine before you change anything else.

**Bottom line:** if you'd miss the taste of coffee, go half-caf or decaf. If you mostly want a warm drink with a mild lift, tea is an easy step down, especially green tea. Herbal tea works for the evenings.
