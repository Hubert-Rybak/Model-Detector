**The Last Watch**

For thirty-one years, Moira Dunne had climbed the hundred and twelve steps of Carrick Point Light, and in thirty-one years no one had ever thanked her.

She didn't mind. Ships passed; ships didn't wreck. That was thanks enough, she told herself, though the telling grew harder once the Commission installed an automatic lamp.

On her last evening, she found a bottle wedged in the rocks below the tower. Green glass, cork swollen tight. Inside, a page torn from a school exercise book.

> *To the person who turns on the lighthouse,*
>
> *I can see your light from my bedroom. When it storms, I count your flashes until my dad's boat comes home. Thank you for not falling asleep.*
>
> *—Ruth, age 8*

It was dated twenty-six years earlier.

Moira sat on the wet stone and read it four times. Ruth would be grown now, with a window of her own somewhere. Maybe her father had retired. Maybe his boat had always come home.

That night she kept watch until dawn, though the machine needed no one.

In the morning, she tore a page from her logbook, wrote three words, corked the bottle, and gave it back to the sea.

*I never did.*
