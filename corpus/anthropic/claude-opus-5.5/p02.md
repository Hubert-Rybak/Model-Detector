Here's the big secret: **a fridge doesn't actually make cold. It moves heat.** It grabs heat from inside the fridge and dumps it out into your kitchen. When you take enough heat away from something, what's left feels cold.

### The trick: evaporation steals heat

Have you ever climbed out of a swimming pool and felt chilly, even on a hot day? The water on your skin is evaporating, which means it's turning from a liquid into a gas. Doing that takes energy, so the water takes heat from your body. Whenever a liquid turns into a gas, it soaks up heat from whatever is around it.

A fridge uses that exact trick, over and over again.

### The heat sponge

A long loop of pipe runs through your fridge. It's filled with a special liquid called **refrigerant**. Water has to get really hot (100°C / 212°F) before it boils, but refrigerant boils even when it's colder than ice. Think of it as a sponge for heat. Here's its trip around the loop:

1. **Inside the fridge,** the cold refrigerant boils into a gas. Just like the pool water on your skin, it soaks up heat while it does that. It takes heat from the air, the milk and the leftover pizza, and that's what makes everything in there cold.
2. **The compressor** is a pump at the bottom of the fridge, and it's what makes that humming sound. It squeezes the gas really tightly, and squeezing a gas heats it up. That's one reason a bike pump gets warm when you pump up a tire.
3. **The coils on the back or bottom of the fridge** carry this hot gas. The gas is hotter than your kitchen, so its heat flows out into the room. As the gas cools, it turns back into a liquid. The sponge has been wrung out!
4. **A super-skinny tube** lets the liquid trickle through into a wider pipe, where the pressure suddenly drops. That makes it really cold again, and it heads back inside to soak up more heat.

Round and round it goes, all day and all night.

The fridge also has thick walls packed with foam. They're like a puffy winter jacket, except their job is to keep heat *out*. A rubbery seal around the door stops warm air from sneaking in. A part called a thermostat switches the compressor on when it gets too warm inside and off again when it's cold enough. That's why the humming starts and stops.

### Try it yourself

- **Feel the evaporation trick:** Lick your finger (or dip it in water) and blow on it. Feel how cold it gets? Your fridge uses that same trick.
- **Feel the heat from your food:** With a grown-up's help, hold your hand *near* (not on) the vent at the bottom of the fridge or the coils on the back. That warm air is heat that used to be inside the fridge!

**Brain-teaser:** If you left the fridge door open on a hot day, would it cool down your kitchen? Nope! It would actually make the kitchen a tiny bit *warmer*. The fridge would just be moving heat from the kitchen back into the kitchen, and its hard-working motor gives off a little extra heat too.
