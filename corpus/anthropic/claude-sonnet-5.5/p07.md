Skipping workouts is really common, and the guilt can make it worse. It turns exercise into something you associate with feeling bad. A few things tend to help.

**1. Figure out why you're skipping.** The fix depends on the cause:
- **Too tired or no time:** Move workouts to a different time of day, or make them shorter.
- **Dreading the workout itself:** You may have picked something you don't enjoy. Try a different type of exercise.
- **All-or-nothing thinking** ("I can't do the full hour, so why bother"): This is the most common one, and the next tip is for it.

**2. Make the bar embarrassingly low.** Commit to 10 minutes, or even just to getting changed and starting. You'll often keep going once you're moving. Even if you don't, you've kept the habit alive. A 10-minute workout beats a perfect plan you never start.

**3. Remove friction.** Lay out your clothes the night before, decide what you're doing in advance, and keep a gym bag in the car. If getting to the gym is the barrier, work out at home.

**4. Schedule it like an appointment.** "Sometime this week" rarely happens, but "Tuesday and Thursday at 7am" does. It also helps to tie it to something you already do, like working out right after work before you sit down.

**5. Give yourself a reason to show up besides "I should."** That could be a podcast you only listen to at the gym, a friend to meet, a class you like, or a good playlist.

**6. Let go of the guilt.** Skipping a workout isn't a moral failure, and beating yourself up doesn't build consistency. A good rule is to never miss twice in a row. If you skip one day, just do the next one.
