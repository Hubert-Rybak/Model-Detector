**The Keeper's Reply**

Marrow Point had given Edwin nineteen years of driftwood, tangled rope, and one very confused seagull, but never a letter.

Then, on a gray March morning, a barnacled green bottle nudged against the landing stairs.

Inside was a page, brittle as onion skin, written in small, careful script:

*To the keeper of any light: I have tended mine for thirty years and never once learned whether anyone sees it. Do the ships look up? Do they care? If you know, please tell me. —Mara, Skerry Light*

Edwin read it three times. The ink had faded, and the bottle had clearly drifted for years; Mara might be long gone. Still, he sat down and wrote on the back of her page.

*Dear Mara, they look up. Last winter a trawler found the channel in a blizzard because of my beam. The captain sent me a ham. I couldn't decide whether to cry or eat it, so I did both. Keep yours burning. Somebody out there needs it. —Edwin, Marrow Point*

He corked the bottle, carried it down to the water, and let the tide take it.

Far out, a freighter slid along the horizon. Edwin waved at it, just in case.
