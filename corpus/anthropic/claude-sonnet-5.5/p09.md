Tea is usually the easier way to cut down. A typical cup has roughly a third to a half the caffeine of a typical cup of coffee, and there's a smooth ladder from there all the way down to zero. Coffee can still work if you control the dose.

**Rough caffeine per 8 oz (240 ml) cup**

| Drink | Caffeine |
|---|---|
| Brewed coffee | 80-120 mg |
| Espresso (per shot) | ~65 mg |
| Black tea | 40-70 mg |
| Matcha (1 tsp powder) | 35-70 mg |
| Oolong | 30-50 mg |
| Green / white tea | 20-45 mg |
| Decaf coffee | 2-15 mg |
| Decaf tea | 2-5 mg |
| Herbal tea (rooibos, chamomile, peppermint, ginger) | 0 mg |

These are ranges because preparation matters a lot, and so does cup size. A 16 oz coffee-shop coffee can run 150-300+ mg, while a mug of green tea might be 30.

**How they differ in practice**

- **Dose per cup:** Tea wins. Even a strong black tea usually lands below an average coffee, so just switching drinks is already a step down.
- **Adjustability:** With tea you have dials. A shorter steep, cooler water, less leaf, or cold-steeping all lower the caffeine. With coffee, the main levers are cup size, half-caf, and decaf.
- **How it feels:** Tea contains L-theanine, which many people find makes the lift feel smoother and less jittery. Coffee hits faster and harder, which some people like, but it makes jitters and an afternoon crash more likely. The theanine evidence is modest, so I'd treat it as a bonus, not a guarantee.
- **Stomach:** Coffee is more acidic and more likely to aggravate reflux. Tea is generally gentler, though strong black tea on an empty stomach bothers some people.
- **Ritual:** For many people this is what actually decides it. If you love the bold, roasty morning cup, tea can feel like a downgrade, and half-caf or decaf coffee might stick better than forcing yourself onto tea.

**Tea traps**

- Matcha is more caffeinated than you'd expect, since you're drinking the whole leaf.
- Chai, English breakfast, and many bottled iced teas are black tea, and some "focus" or "energy" blends have added caffeine. Yerba mate and guayusa count as "herbal" but are caffeinated.
- Four or five cups of tea can add up to as much as two coffees.

**A simple step-down plan**

1. Swap your *latest* cup first (afternoon coffee to tea). Caffeine's half-life is about 5 hours, so a 3 p.m. coffee is still around half-strength at 8 p.m. Try to finish caffeine at least 8 hours before bed.
2. Then work down the ladder: coffee, half-caf, black tea, green or white tea, then decaf or herbal.
3. Taper over a week or two rather than quitting cold. Withdrawal (headache, fatigue, irritability, brain fog) typically starts 12-24 hours after your last dose, peaks in the first couple of days, and fades within about a week. Cutting roughly 25% every few days usually keeps it mild.

**Bottom line**

- **Lowest-friction cut:** Switch to tea. Use black tea if you still need a kick, then green or white, then herbal.
- **Love coffee, just want less:** Keep it, but go smaller or half-caf, and make everything after noon decaf.
- **Zero caffeine but a warm ritual:** Rooibos (malty, takes milk well) or decaf, in a mug you like.
