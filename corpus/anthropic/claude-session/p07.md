The feeling bad part is probably doing more damage than the skipping. Guilt makes exercise feel like a debt you owe, and people avoid their debts.

A few things that tend to actually work:

**Shrink the unit.** If the workout you keep skipping is 45 minutes, the thing you're avoiding is 45 minutes. Make the commitment ten minutes. Ten minutes is hard to argue with, and about half the time you'll keep going once you've started.

**Attach it to something that already happens.** "After I make coffee, I do ten squats" survives bad weeks far better than "I'll go to the gym at some point."

**Decide the night before, in detail.** What, where, what you'll wear. Decisions made at 7 a.m. by a tired brain default to no.

**Count showing up, not performance.** A short, lazy session still counts. The habit is the goal; intensity can come later.

**Pick something you don't hate.** If you dread the thing, that's information, not a character flaw. Walking, climbing, swimming, dancing in the kitchen all count.

And when you miss a day, the rule is "never two in a row." One miss is noise. Two starts to be a pattern.
