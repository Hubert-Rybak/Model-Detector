If the goal is less caffeine without giving up the ritual, tea is the easier road, mostly because of the numbers.

A typical 240 ml cup of brewed coffee has around 80 to 100 mg of caffeine. Black tea lands around 40 to 50 mg, green tea around 25 to 35 mg, and white tea often less than that. So swapping coffee for black tea roughly halves your intake per cup, and green tea cuts it by two thirds or more, before you change anything else.

Tea also delivers its caffeine differently. It contains L-theanine, an amino acid that smooths out the stimulant effect, so people often describe tea as alert-but-calm where coffee can feel like a spike. That makes the step down less jarring.

Practical ways to use this:

- Replace your second coffee of the day with black tea first, then work backwards to the first cup. Going cold turkey tends to produce headaches for a few days; a gradual swap usually doesn't.
- Brew tea for a shorter time. Caffeine extracts quickly, but a two-minute steep still pulls noticeably less than five.
- Decaf coffee is a real option if what you miss is the taste. It still has a few milligrams, not zero, but close.
- Herbal infusions (rooibos, peppermint, chamomile) have no caffeine at all and keep the hot-cup habit intact.

One caution: a large mug of strong black tea can approach a weak coffee, so "tea" isn't automatically low. Watch the steep time and the cup size more than the label.
