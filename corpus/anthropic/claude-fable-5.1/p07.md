First, the feeling bad part is worth looking at on its own, because it's probably making things worse. Guilt turns "workout" into a loaded thing you'd rather not think about, and not thinking about it is exactly how you end up skipping it again. Most people who get consistent aren't more disciplined than you; they've just made the whole thing less of an event.

A few things that tend to actually help:

**Make the minimum embarrassingly small.** If the plan is "45 minutes at the gym," skipping is easy because it's a big ask. If the plan is "put on shoes and do 10 minutes," you can almost always do that, and about half the time you'll keep going. The win is showing up, not the session.

**Be honest about whether you like the workout.** A lot of skipping is really "I don't want to do *that*." If you dread the thing itself, swap it. Walking, a short bodyweight circuit, a class with other people, something you can do while listening to a podcast. Consistency with something mediocre beats sporadic bursts of the perfect program.

**Cut the decision out.** Same days, same time, clothes laid out, attached to something you already do (right after coffee, right after work). Every decision point is a chance to bail, so remove as many as you can.

**Treat a skip as information, not a verdict.** "What got in the way?" is a useful question. "What's wrong with me?" isn't. Usually the answer is boring and fixable: too tired at that hour, the gym is too far, you had no plan for what to do once you got there.

**Don't make up for missed days.** Doubling the next session just makes the next session scarier. A missed day costs you almost nothing; quitting for three weeks because you missed a day costs a lot.
