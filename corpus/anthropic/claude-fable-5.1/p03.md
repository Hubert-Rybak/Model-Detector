**The Keeper's Reply**

Every morning for thirty-one years, Elias Hartwell walked the shingle below Carrick Point, collecting what the tide left behind. Driftwood. Rope. Once, a wooden horse with its paint worn to ghost-colors.

The bottle was thick green glass, stoppered with wax. Inside, a page folded small.

He opened it at the kitchen table, kettle ticking as it cooled.

*To whoever finds this: I am twelve and my father says the sea goes everywhere. So I am sending this everywhere. Please write back. — Mara, Port Ellis*

The date at the top was forty years old.

Elias did the arithmetic. Fifty-two now, if she had lived. Port Ellis was two hundred miles down the coast, a town he had passed through once, long ago, on the way to somewhere else.

He sat there a long while. Then he took out paper and wrote: *Dear Mara, it came to me. The sea does go everywhere. It just takes its time.*

He addressed it to the post office, general delivery, knowing it would probably go nowhere.

That evening, climbing the tower to light the lamp, he found he was humming. The beam swept out across the dark water, searching, patient, the way it always had.
