**Short version:** tea almost always has less caffeine than coffee, and it's a much easier drink to taper with, because it comes in a natural ladder from fairly strong (black) down to zero (herbal). Coffee is more of an on/off switch unless you go half-caf or decaf.

**Caffeine per 8 oz cup (approximate)**

| Drink | Caffeine |
|---|---|
| Cold brew | 100–200+ mg |
| Drip coffee | 80–100 mg |
| Matcha | 60–80 mg |
| Espresso (1 shot, ~1 oz) | ~65 mg |
| Black tea | 40–70 mg |
| Oolong | 30–50 mg |
| Green tea | 25–45 mg |
| White tea | 15–40 mg |
| Decaf coffee | 2–7 mg |
| Decaf tea | 2–5 mg |
| Herbal (chamomile, peppermint, rooibos) | 0 mg |

Two caveats. A coffee-shop "medium" is usually 16 oz, so one cup is often 200–300 mg. And tea varies a lot with brewing: more leaf, hotter water, and longer steeping all pull out more caffeine, so a strong, long-steeped black tea can rival a weak coffee.

**Why tea tends to feel gentler**

Tea contains L-theanine, an amino acid that seems to take the edge off caffeine. People typically describe tea as a calmer, more even alertness rather than a spike and crash. That's a real pattern, but the effect is modest; a strong black tea will not feel like water.

**A practical taper**

Quitting abruptly usually means headaches, fatigue, and irritability for a few days (withdrawal peaks around day 1–2). Stepping down avoids most of that:

1. Swap the afternoon coffee for black tea; keep the morning one.
2. Move the morning coffee to half-caf (mix regular and decaf) or a smaller cup.
3. Go black tea → green tea → white or decaf tea over a couple of weeks.
4. Land on herbal or decaf to keep the ritual.

Keep a cutoff time whatever you drink. Caffeine's half-life is roughly 5 hours, so a 3 pm cup is still half-active at 8 pm.

**Things that trip people up**

- Matcha and yerba mate are not low-caffeine options, even though they're sold as "tea."
- Decaf isn't zero, but it's close enough for most people.
- The "rinse the leaves for 30 seconds to decaffeinate" trick removes maybe 10–20%, not most of it.
- Tea's tannins can reduce iron absorption when you drink it with meals, which matters mainly if your iron is already low.

**Bottom line:** if you want to keep coffee, go half-caf or decaf and shrink the cup. If you're open to switching, tea gives you a smoother ride and more rungs on the way down. Most people find green or white tea a comfortable landing spot that still gives a little lift.
