Here's the secret: a refrigerator doesn't actually *make* cold. There's no such thing as "cold stuff" you can pump into a box. What a fridge really does is **grab heat from inside and dump it outside**, over and over. Less heat inside = colder food.

So how do you grab heat? With a trick you already know.

**The trick: evaporating things steal heat**

When you climb out of a swimming pool, you feel chilly, even on a hot day. That's because the water on your skin is evaporating (turning into vapor), and to do that it has to *steal heat* from your skin. Sweat works the same way. Evaporation is nature's way of cooling things down.

A fridge is a machine built around that trick.

**What's inside**

Hidden in the walls of your fridge is a loop of pipe filled with a special liquid called a *refrigerant*. It's picked because it boils (turns into gas) at a really low temperature, way below freezing. The liquid goes round and round the loop in four steps:

1. **Inside the fridge: soak up heat.** The refrigerant squirts through a tiny opening into the pipes inside the fridge. That makes it spread out and evaporate, just like pool water on your skin, and as it does, it sucks heat out of the air and the food. Your leftover pizza gets colder.

2. **The pump squeezes it.** Now the refrigerant is a gas carrying all that stolen heat. A pump called the *compressor* (that's the humming you hear) squeezes the gas hard. Squeezing a gas makes it hot. You can feel this if you pump up a bike tire fast: the pump gets warm.

3. **Outside the fridge: dump the heat.** The hot gas flows through the coils on the back or bottom of the fridge. Since it's hotter than your kitchen, the heat leaks out into the room, and the gas cools down and turns back into a liquid.

4. **Back to the start.** The liquid heads back to the tiny opening and the whole thing repeats. Thousands of times a day.

So the heat from your food ends up... in your kitchen. Go put your hand near the back or bottom of the fridge. It's warm! That warmth is literally the heat that used to be in your juice.

**Two more pieces**

- **Thick walls.** The fridge has puffy foam inside its walls and a rubber seal around the door so heat from the kitchen can't sneak back in easily. (That's why grown-ups groan when you stand there with the door open: you're letting heat in and making the pump work harder.)
- **A thermostat.** A little temperature sensor turns the pump on when it gets too warm inside and off when it's cold enough. That's the click you sometimes hear.

**The one-sentence version:** a fridge is a heat-moving machine. It uses a liquid that evaporates inside (stealing heat from your food) and gets squeezed back into a liquid outside (dumping that heat into the kitchen), round and round forever.
